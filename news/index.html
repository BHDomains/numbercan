Bin news, and what it means for your bin

Short takes, written here. The full rule coverage - deadlines, fines, every source - lives at our sibling site, cans.nyc.

September 17, 2026

A council member wants the bin fines paused. Label either way.

Council Member Simcha Felder has asked DSNY to suspend the new bin fines, citing shortages, resale markups and - our subject - stolen bins. DSNY has not paused anything; the fines are still live.

Notice what made his list: bins walking off. Whatever City Hall decides about fines, the fix for that part sits with the owner, not the agency. DSNY does not replace stolen bins. A big address on the side and a photographed serial number are still the only things that make a bin provably yours.

Full coverage: cans.nyc

September 8, 2026

Fines are live - and every bin on the curb is now identical

The warning period ended on Labor Day. DSNY is now ticketing 1-9 unit buildings that set out trash in anything but the official bin, starting at $50.

That settled the compliance question - and created the identification one. Roughly 750,000 buildings now put out the same bin, same shape, same lid. Fines are about which bin you buy. Mix-ups are about which bin comes back. The first one is the city's job to explain; the second is a label.

Full coverage: cans.nyc

September 8, 2026

The free-bin reimbursement window has closed

STAR homeowners had been eligible for a reimbursement covering one 45-gallon bin. That window closed with the warning period. From here, everyone buys their own - about $45 to $55 depending on size.

Which makes the math simple: a bin is now a $50 object you own, with no replacement policy if it disappears. Marking it costs a fraction of replacing it.
